Start With Your Home Equity

Everything starts with understanding your home equity. Home equity is generally the difference between the estimated current value of your home and the remaining mortgage balance.

Estimated Home Equity = Estimated Home Value − Mortgage Balance

Example: estimated home value $450,000 − mortgage balance $275,000 = estimated equity $175,000.

Important: this does not mean a homeowner can necessarily borrow $175,000. Available borrowing depends on lender requirements, loan-to-value limits, credit profile, income, property characteristics and other underwriting factors. This is educational information, not an approval.

What Is a HELOC?

HELOC stands for Home Equity Line of Credit. It is generally a revolving line of credit secured by the homeowner's property. Unlike receiving one lump sum, a HELOC can let the borrower access funds as needed during the draw period, subject to the HELOC's terms and the available credit on the line.

Because a HELOC is built on home equity, the size and availability of the line depend on how much equity you have. Before estimating what a HELOC could look like, understand your home equity.

How Does a HELOC Work?

The HELOC lifecycle usually follows a sequence of stages. The exact details vary by lender and product, so treat this as the general shape of how a HELOC works rather than a fixed set of terms:

  1. Establish available credit based on your equity and the lender's underwriting
  2. Enter the draw period
  3. Borrow as needed, up to the available credit
  4. Make the required payments
  5. The draw period ends
  6. The repayment period begins
  7. Repay outstanding principal and interest according to the loan terms

Lender-specific terms are not universal. Draw periods, repayment structures and payment rules differ, so always check the terms of the specific HELOC you are considering.

HELOC Draw Period

The HELOC draw period is the window during which you can access the available funds on your line. During this time you can typically make multiple draws when permitted, repay what you use, and in many cases borrow again. Payments during the draw period may be structured in different ways depending on the lender and product.

It matters to know when the draw period ends, because that transition changes how the line behaves. Exact durations and terms vary by lender and product — there is no single standard.

HELOC Interest-Only Payments

Some HELOCs may have payment structures during the draw period that allow or require interest-only payments. In that structure, a payment during the draw period is primarily based on interest on the amount you have drawn, rather than reducing principal. In short, a HELOC interest only payment covers the cost of borrowing on what you have drawn, rather than paying down what you owe.

Why this matters:

A lower payment during the draw period does not necessarily represent the eventual repayment-period payment. When repayment begins and principal is included, the payment can rise.

Not every HELOC operates identically. Whether interest-only payments are available, and under what conditions, depends on the lender and the specific loan terms.

HELOC Payment After Draw Period

One of the most useful concepts to understand is the payment transition. When the draw period ends, the structure of the payment can change:

During the draw period

A payment may be interest-focused, which keeps it lower.

After the draw period

Repayment generally includes principal + interest, so the monthly payment may increase.

This potential increase is sometimes described as payment shock. It is a normal feature of how some HELOCs are structured, and it is simply worth understanding in advance so you can plan for it.

HELOC Requirements

Lenders may consider several factors when evaluating a HELOC. At a high level these can include:

  • available home equity
  • combined loan-to-value
  • credit profile
  • income
  • existing debt obligations
  • the property itself
  • occupancy
  • lender-specific underwriting requirements

Specific thresholds vary by lender and are not universal, so we do not publish approval criteria or tell you whether you qualify. What we can say is that the first measurable variable is home equity.

Common HELOC questions

How does a HELOC work in simple terms?
It turns available home equity into a revolving credit line you can draw from during the draw period and repay over time, instead of receiving a single lump sum.
How long is a HELOC draw period?
It varies by lender and product — there is no universal duration. Check the terms of the specific line you are considering.
Is a HELOC interest-only payment always available?
No. Some HELOCs allow or require interest-focused payments during the draw period; others do not. It depends on the lender and loan terms.
Does the HELOC payment after the draw period increase?
It can. When repayment begins and principal is included, the monthly payment may be higher than the interest-focused payment during the draw period.
What are common HELOC requirements?
Lenders may look at available equity, combined loan-to-value, credit profile, income, debts, the property and occupancy, among other underwriting factors. Thresholds vary.
How much equity do I have?
Estimate it as your home value minus your mortgage balance, then confirm your situation with the Home Equity Calculator.
